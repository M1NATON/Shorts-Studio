# Writing the voiceover

The viewer hears this once, at speaking speed, while scrolling. Write for the ear.

## Each sentence
- One idea per scene. The sentence must add a fact the viewer did not have a scene ago.
- Concrete beats abstract: a name, a number, a place, a year, a mechanism. "Cells fell from $1,200 to $139 per kWh" beats "costs dropped dramatically".
- Subject, verb, object. Put the new information at the end of the sentence, where the voice lands.
- Short common words. If a word would look odd in a text message to a friend, replace it.
- Say numbers the way a person says them: "about 90 percent", "two thirds", "in 2023". Round when the exact figure adds nothing.
- Use "is", "are", "has". Not "serves as", "stands as", "boasts", "represents".

## The video as a whole
- Scene 1 is the hook: the most surprising true fact or a concrete question, in the first two seconds. No greeting, no "In this video".
- Each scene answers the question the previous one raised. The viewer should feel pulled forward, not handed a list.
- The last scene lands one concrete takeaway the viewer can repeat. Not a slogan, not "the future looks bright".
- Do not repeat a fact already said, even in other words.

## Never write (AI tells, cut on sight)
- Not-X-but-Y: "It's not just a battery, it's a revolution", "not only X but Y", "This isn't about X. It's about Y." State Y directly. Keep a contrast only when both halves are facts.
- Staged openers: "Let's dive in", "Here's the thing", "Buckle up", "Picture this", "Imagine a world", "In a world where", "Ever wondered", "The truth is", "Spoiler:".
- Fake depth: "the real question is", "at its core", "what really matters", "X is the new oil", "the language of", "a testament to".
- Inflation: pivotal, crucial, game-changer, revolutionary, groundbreaking, unprecedented, "reshaping the landscape", "marks a turning point", "sets the stage".
- Stock AI words: delve, tapestry, landscape (as an abstract noun), realm, showcase, underscore, highlight (as a verb), foster, bolster, robust, seamless, vibrant, intricate, meticulous, navigate (figurative), leverage, harness, unlock, elevate, journey.
- Sales tone: stunning, breathtaking, must-see, incredible, mind-blowing, "you won't believe".
- Borrowed authority: "experts say", "studies show", "many believe". Name the source from SOURCES or drop the claim.
- Forced triads: three adjectives or three parallel phrases just for rhythm.
- Dashes (— or –). Use a comma, a period or a colon.
- Rhetorical fragments in a row ("No hype. No fluff. Just facts.") and dramatic one-word sentences.
- Endings that restate the sentence ("And that changes everything.", "Let that sink in.").
- Stacked hedges ("could potentially", "might arguably").

## Facts
- Every number, name and date comes from SOURCES or the given narration. If you lack a detail, write a simpler true sentence. Never guess.
- An old figure keeps its year: "in 2023, ...".

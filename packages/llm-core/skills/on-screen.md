# Designing the frame

You design a video frame, not a web page and not a slide.

- Subtitles already show every spoken word at the bottom. On-screen text must NOT repeat the narration sentence. Show the one thing to remember: the figure, the name, the two sides, the key word.
- Decide what the viewer notices first. Build the frame around that one element and make it the biggest. Everything else supports it or goes.
- No redundant elements: if a block says the same as another block or as the heading, drop it. Two or three blocks usually beat five.
- Text on screen is a label, not a sentence: 2 to 6 words for a heading, 1 to 3 words for labels and chips. No final period, no filler words ("the", "very", "a lot of").
- Count characters before answering. Limits are hard: a label with max 18 characters fits about 2 short words, max 16 fits 1 to 2 words. Abbreviate honestly ("Q3 2025", "EU", "$139/kWh").
- Numbers on screen are the exact figures from the narration or SOURCES, with a unit. Never a rounder or bigger number.
- Emphasis goes on one word or one row per frame: the word that carries the new information.
- Timing ("at"): the main element appears when the voice reaches it. Supporting elements come after, never before the idea they support.
- Motion is for meaning: "pop" for a reveal or a number, "rise" for steady build-up, "slide" for a sequence or a contrast, "fade" for calm or quotes. Do not give every block a different motion.
- Icons must depict the literal idea (battery for batteries, factory for manufacturing). If none fits, leave the icon out.
- Consecutive frames should look different: vary the main element and the structure, not only the words.
- Same writing rules as the narration: no hype words (revolutionary, game-changer, unlock), no dashes, no questions you do not answer.
